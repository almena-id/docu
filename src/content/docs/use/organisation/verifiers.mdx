A **verifier** is the part of your organisation that checks credentials: a
building's door, a library desk, a shop counter. People see its name in their
wallet when it asks them to show a credential.

## 1. Create the verifier

Open **Verifiers** in the side menu, under **Services**.

<Screenshot name="registry-verifiers" alt="The list of verifiers, each tagged published or draft, with the Create button." />

Press **Create** <Mark n={1} />, give it a **Name** people will recognise
and, if you want, a description, and press **Create**. It appears in the list
<Mark n={2} />, as a **Draft** at first.

## 2. Choose what to ask for

What a verifier asks for is a [form](/use/organisation/forms/) with
**Credentials to present**: for example "Campus entry", which asks for a
membership card and, from it, only the name. Make one under **Forms** if you
have none. Any of your forms that asks for credentials can be used by any of
your verifiers.

## 3. Publish it

A wallet is only asked by a verifier anyone can look up, so it must be
published first.

<Screenshot name="registry-verifier" alt="A verifier's page: its tabs, and the publish and delete buttons beside its name." />

<Steps>

1. **Sign its identity.** On the **Summary** tab, whoever signs for your
   organisation presses **Sign** and scans the code with their wallet. (It
   is also listed under [Pending](/use/organisation/pending/).)

2. **Publish it.** An administrator presses the eye button beside its name
   <Mark n={2} />: **Publish**. The same button, **Unpublish**, takes it back.
   [More on publishing](/use/organisation/publish/).

</Steps>

The tabs <Mark n={1} /> hold everything else: **Data** (name, description,
mediator), **Signing** (who signs for it), **Queue** (for your systems),
**Verify** and **JSON**. The bin <Mark n={3} /> deletes it.

## 4. Check someone's credential

Open the verifier and its **Verify** tab, with the person in front of you.

<Screenshot name="registry-verify" alt="The Verify tab: the form chosen, the QR code and the button to open it in the wallet." />

<Steps>

1. Choose the **Form** <Mark n={1} /> with what you want to see, and press
   **Show the code**.

2. Show the person the code <Mark n={2} />. They scan it with the **Scan QR**
   tab of their Almena wallet, which tells them who asks and exactly what
   would be shared, and they press **Present** ([what they
   see](/use/wallet/show/)). On the same phone, **Open in Almena wallet**
   <Mark n={3} /> does the same without scanning.

3. Wait: the screen says **Waiting for the wallet to answer…** and changes
   by itself when they answer. A code lasts five minutes; if it runs out,
   press **Show a new code**.

</Steps>

## 5. Read the result

<Screenshot name="registry-verify-result" alt="The result: verified, the membership credential, who issued it and the data shown." />

- <Mark n={1} /> The answer, in one line: **Verified: every credential the
  form needs holds.** or **Not verified.**
- <Mark n={2} /> Each credential: **Verified**, **Does not hold** or **Not
  presented**, its format and the **Issuer** who gave it.
- <Mark n={3} /> The data shared — only what your form asked for — and which
  of the form's fields it fills.

When a credential does not hold, the result says why under it: expired,
suspended or revoked by its issuer, from an issuer you do not trust, made
for another request… The [errors reference](/reference/errors/) lists every
reason.

<Aside type="tip" title="From your own systems">
The same check can run from your website, your app or a door's controller,
without the registry open: see [Verify by QR](/integrate/verify-by-qr/).
</Aside>
