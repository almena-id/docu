Think of the cards and papers you carry: your degree, your membership card,
a letter from your employer. Someone gave them to you, you keep them, and you
show them when you are asked. Almena does the same, with digital ones.

## Three parties

- **The organisation** — a university, a club, a company, a city hall —
  gives you a **credential**: a digital card that says something about you,
  such as "has a degree in Law" or "is a member since 2024". It signs it, the
  way it would stamp a paper.
- **You** keep it in the **Almena wallet**, an app on your phone or computer.
  It is yours: nobody else holds a copy, not even Almena.
- **Whoever asks** — an employer, the door of a building, a website — asks you
  to show it. Your wallet tells you exactly what will be shown, and nothing
  goes without your OK.

The one who asks can tell, in a second, that the credential is genuine,
that the organisation really gave it, and that it has not been withdrawn.
There is no need to call the organisation, and the organisation does not
learn where you showed it.

## The pieces of Almena

You will meet these names in the rest of the documentation:

| Name | What it is | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wallet** | The app where you keep your credentials and show them. | Everyone |
| **Catalog** ([catalog.almena.id](https://catalog.almena.id)) | The shop window: every credential you can ask for, and from whom. | Everyone |
| **Registry** ([registry.almena.id](https://registry.almena.id)) | Where organisations set themselves up, receive applications and give credentials. | Organisations |
| **Mediator** | A mailbox that keeps messages for your wallet while it is off. | Nobody directly: the wallet uses it |
